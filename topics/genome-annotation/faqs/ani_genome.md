** ANI (Average Nucleotide Identity)** is a common measure for evaluation of the similarity of two prokaryote genomes at the DNA sequence level.
ANI is defined as ** the average percentage of matching nucleotides between all shared genomic regions of two organisms.** 
Matching nucleotides can be computed using different comparison or alignment methods. 
In genome comparison, ANI is commonly used to:
- Estimate how closely related two microbes are
- Help decide whether two strains belong to the same species
- Replace older lab-based DNA–DNA hybridization methods.
Two bacterial genomes with ≥95–96% ANI are usually considered the same species.

ANI is less commonly used for eukaryotes because it is harder to apply for larger genomes with many repeats/duplications and non-coding regions.

** Important: ANI is also mainly reliable for very close genomes.**
